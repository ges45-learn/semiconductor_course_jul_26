## 1.3 Semiconductor value chain: An introduction 
* **Upstream:** Design and IP
  * Designing a chip to meet a demanding application is one of the most complex challenges in engineering. It requires aligning technical innovation with strict constraints on performance, power, cost, and time-to-market. Every phase — from architecture to verification to physical layout — must be executed with precision, often under intense pressure and with little room for error. So, what does it truly take to design a chip that meets a demanding specification?
  * What advantages do Systems-on-a-Chip (SoC) offer compared to traditional chip design, and how might these benefits influence design choices for your mobile or embedded devices?
* **Midstream:** Manufacturing and fabrication
  * Why do you think such extreme cleanliness is required in semiconductor fabrication?
  * How might even microscopic contamination affect chip performance or yield?
  * How does the Process Design Kit (PDK) help bridge the gap between chip designers and foundry engineers?
  * Why is this collaboration critical for successful fabrication? 
* **Downstream:** Assembly, Testing, and Packaging
  * Why do you think yield drops significantly when moving to more advanced technology nodes?
  * What trade-offs might companies face when deciding whether to adopt a newer node?

## 1.5 Moore's Law (1965–2000) [10 µm --> 0.130 µm]

* **Read Article:** ["Driving Semiconductor Innovation: Moore's Law at Fairchild and Intel"](https://doi.org/10.1017/eso.2020.38)
* **Read Article:** ["Cramming More Components onto Integrated Circuits"](https://www.computerhistory.org/collections/catalog/102770822/)
  * Consider how Gordon Moore's vision influenced innovation, manufacturing, and the pace of technological advancement over the decades.
  * **Key Innovations:**
    * **Upstream:** Field-effect transistor (FET)
    * **Midstream:** Photolithography
  * **Questions to Consider:**
    * What specific applications and devices did Moore foresee as a result of integrated circuits, and what did he believe was the "biggest potential" of this technology?
    * Examine the graph in the "Cost and Curves" section of the article. What relationship does it show between the number of components on a chip and the manufacturing cost per component?
    * At what complexity level does Moore predict the minimum cost per component will be reached by 1970?
    * Moore anticipates a thermal/heat dissipation problem as the component count increases. How does he argue that this will not be an insurmountable issue for integrated circuits (ICs)?
